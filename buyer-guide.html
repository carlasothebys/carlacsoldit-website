Your Complete 2026 Guide

How to Buy Luxury Real Estate in
Jupiter & Palm Beach County

From off-market access to golf club memberships — everything you need to know before you buy, from an agent with 29 years of local expertise.

1

Define Your Lifestyle & Budget

Before you start touring homes, get crystal clear on what you actually want your life to look like. Palm Beach County offers vastly different lifestyles depending on where you buy — and the right neighborhood makes all the difference.

  • Waterfront vs. Golf Community — Do you want to be on the water (ocean, Intracoastal, river) or inside a private golf club? Many buyers want both — communities like Admirals Cove offer both marina and golf.
  • Privacy vs. Community — Gated communities like Bears Club and Loxahatchee Club offer extreme privacy. Neighborhoods like Tequesta and Hobe Sound offer a more relaxed, village feel.
  • Primary vs. Second Home — If this is a primary residence, schools, commute, and everyday amenities matter. If it's a seasonal home, maximizing lifestyle features takes priority.
  • Budget — All-In — Factor in HOA fees ($500–$3,000+/month), club initiation fees ($85K–$475K), annual dues, insurance, and property taxes. Your "all-in" monthly cost can be 40–60% above your mortgage payment.
Carla's Tip: The best buyers I work with come prepared with a clear vision. Write down your top 5 must-haves and top 5 nice-to-haves before we meet. It saves months of searching.
2

Get Pre-Qualified Before You Look

In the luxury market, pre-qualification isn't optional — it's expected. Sellers of $3M+ properties will not entertain offers from unverified buyers. Here's what you need:

  • Proof of Funds — For all-cash purchases (very common in luxury real estate), provide a bank or brokerage statement showing available funds.
  • Pre-Approval Letter — If financing, use a private bank (JP Morgan Private Bank, Goldman Sachs, First Republic, Signature) that understands luxury real estate. Standard mortgage lenders often can't move fast enough.
  • 1031 Exchange — If you're rolling proceeds from another sale, have your qualified intermediary in place before you start shopping.
Carla's Tip: I can connect you with the best private bankers and attorneys in Palm Beach County who specialize in luxury transactions. These relationships matter — they help deals close faster and smoother.

5

Understand Golf Club Memberships

If you're buying in a private golf or country club community, understanding the full membership picture is critical. Many buyers are surprised to learn that membership is NOT included in the purchase price — it's an additional cost.

  • Initiation Fees — Range from $85,000 (Eastpointe) to $475,000 (Admirals Cove Full Golf). Some clubs (like Frenchman's Creek) require you to be a resident to join.
  • Annual Dues — Range from $16,000 to $53,000+ per year, plus food minimums, trail fees, locker fees, and more.
  • Equity vs. Non-Equity — Equity memberships (BallenIsles, Mirasol) refund 75–80% of your initiation when you sell. Non-equity memberships do not.
  • Waitlists — Some clubs (Bears Club, Loxahatchee) have waitlists. Budget 6–24 months if you want membership at a specific club.
  • Outside Members — Some clubs allow non-residents to join. This is important if you're considering a home outside the gates.

6

Make a Winning Offer — Fast

The luxury market in Palm Beach County moves quickly. Average days on market in Jupiter is just 18 days. The best properties often receive multiple offers. Here's how to win:

  • Clean offer — Minimize contingencies where possible. Cash offers with a short inspection period are most competitive.
  • Escalation clause — In multiple-offer situations, an escalation clause ensures you stay competitive without overpaying.
  • Flexible closing — Sellers often have a preferred closing timeline. Matching it wins goodwill.
  • Personal letter — In some cases, a personal letter from the buyer explaining why they love the home can make the difference between two similar offers.
  • Strong earnest money — 5–10% earnest money deposits signal serious intent in luxury transactions.
Carla's Tip: I've been negotiating luxury real estate in this market for 29 years. I know when to be aggressive, when to hold back, and how to structure an offer that wins without leaving money on the table.
7

Due Diligence & Closing

Once your offer is accepted, the due diligence period begins. In Florida, this is typically 10–15 days for luxury properties. Here's what to check:

  • Inspection — Hire a qualified luxury home inspector. Pay special attention to roof age, HVAC systems, seawall condition (for waterfront), and hurricane impact windows/doors.
  • Flood Zone — Verify FEMA flood zone status. Waterfront properties in AE or VE zones require flood insurance (can be significant).
  • HOA Documents — Review at least 3 years of meeting minutes, financials, and rules. Look for special assessments or pending litigation.
  • Title Search — Use a reputable Palm Beach County title company to ensure clean title.
  • Insurance — Get homeowner's insurance quotes before closing. Florida insurance has changed significantly — some areas are challenging to insure at reasonable rates.
